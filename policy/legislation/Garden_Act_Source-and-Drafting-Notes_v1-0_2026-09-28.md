# Garden Act Source and Drafting Notes

September 28, 2026 • Companion to Garden Act version 1.0

## The selected model

Phillip Galinsky directed that Garden nations should be able to exercise the governmental powers of the Mohegan Tribe and the Mashantucket Pequot Tribal Nation. The bill therefore provides actual legislative, executive, fiscal, judicial, and public-safety authority. The earlier alternative of a private association limited to enterprise management is not the selected model.

Both model tribes have operating governmental institutions. That institutional experience supports using the model. It does not establish that extending comparable status to newly constituted descendant nations has already been upheld by a court. Version 1.0 is a legislative proposal requiring legislative-counsel review before introduction; it is not a statement that the proposed legal status already exists.

## Answers about the two tribes

| Question | Evidence and scope |
|---|---|
| Do they impose taxes? | Yes. Mashantucket Pequot publishes a tax system covering sales, lodging, food and beverages, admissions, home ownership, and other categories. Mohegan publishes a taxation code; its 2016 SEC-filed land lease expressly describes meals, hotel occupancy, sales, and admissions taxes. These sources do not establish that either tribe currently imposes a general personal income tax on every member. Rates and exemptions should be checked separately before quoting them. [1–3] |
| Do they have sovereign immunity? | Yes. Mashantucket Pequot Title 53 states the rule and conditions for waiver. Mohegan has adopted limited waivers for specified tort claims. The Supreme Court's Mohegan-related decision in Lewis v. Clarke distinguishes a tribe's immunity from a personal-capacity claim against an employee; indemnification alone does not extend the tribe's immunity to that claim. Immunity concerns amenability to suit, not a general license to disregard applicable law. [4–6] |
| Do they have civil courts? | Yes. Mohegan operates a Tribal Court and a Gaming Disputes Court, each with trial and appellate levels; its Council of Elders has an additional review role for Tribal Court decisions. Mashantucket Pequot expressly describes civil adjudication and a Court of Appeals, as well as criminal cases within its jurisdiction. [7–8] |
| Do they operate public safety? | Yes. Their official materials identify police and fire or emergency services. Mashantucket Pequot describes mutual-aid agreements with local communities. The scope of enforcement is governed by jurisdiction and applicable agreements; it is not authority over everyone in neighboring towns. [9–10] |
| Are tribal members exempt from federal income tax? | Not generally. IRS guidance distinguishes governmental treatment from individual income. Membership alone does not exempt ordinary wages or all distributions. Federal employment and some excise taxes also remain relevant to governmental entities. [11–13] |

|---|---|

| Police, fire, emergency medical services, and criminal courts | Section 405 |

The bill confers comparable powers expressly. It does not declare African descendants to be Native American Indians or silently extend every statute concerning Indian tribes. It preserves existing Indian nations' lands, treaties, program resources, and compacts. This distinction concerns the source of legal authority; it does not reduce the chosen model to an ordinary private corporation.

The comparison is not a claim of exact equivalence in every legal outcome. The bill's direct federal remedies, consensual land-designation conditions, statutory source of criminal authority, and specified federal exceptions require their own analysis. A parity report in section 402 identifies future differences for Congress; it cannot itself create a missing power.

## Constitutional and jurisdictional work for legislative counsel

Morton v. Mancari concerned the particular federal relationship with recognized Indian tribes. Rice v. Cayetano shows that ancestry can function as a racial classification in a voting system. Omitting a race question from an enrollment form therefore does not by itself resolve constitutional scrutiny. Neither decision adjudicates this proposed Garden framework. [14–15]

Section 102 states the proposed remedial foundation under the Thirteenth Amendment and other applicable powers. Congress's authority concerning the badges and incidents of slavery supplies a relevant line of analysis, not a settled holding approving descendant-based sovereign governments or exclusive licenses. Before introduction, counsel should develop the legislative record and examine equal protection, ancestry-based public citizenship, the limits of the Thirteenth Amendment, and the interaction of State consent with federal authority. [16]

Criminal adjudication requires particular attention. Sections 405 and 1202 incorporate rights, limit territory and persons, provide counsel and habeas review, and preserve constitutional limits. Counsel must examine Article III, jury requirements, the source of prosecutorial authority, double jeopardy, and whether each proposed delegation is constitutionally available. Indian tribes' retained sovereignty cannot simply be assumed for a statutory Garden nation. The reference to 25 U.S.C. 1304 is a proposed express extension with corresponding restrictions, not a statement that existing law already authorizes it. [17–18]

Civil, tax, and policing authority must attach to identified persons, transactions, and lands. A commercial district is a licensing boundary and can be larger than the nation’s jurisdictional lands. The bill does not make every district resident a citizen or subject all neighboring property to national taxes or courts. Jurisdictional compacts identify which State functions continue.

## Fidelity to the Garden proposal

The retained structure comes from garden.docx: voluntary founding affiliation; an evidence-based historic roster; closed founding rolls before commercial awards; nations choosing their own institutions; a coordinating Assembly without power over its members; sealed preferences followed by free negotiation; voluntary State participation; conditional regional exclusivity; and repayment of a founding advance.

The following provisions make the proposal operational or qualify claims that cannot be guaranteed:

1. Existing State-authorized businesses count when testing whether a market is closed. Federal prohibition alone cannot erase an actual existing State license or operating business. Geographic boundaries cannot be manipulated to manufacture eligibility.
2. Direct displacement of protected rights is barred, and indirect market effects must be assessed. The bill does not assert that a new enterprise can never affect another business's customers.
3. Sovereign tax powers are available, but no particular national tax is compulsory. Federal governmental treatment does not imply that every citizen or private investor is tax exempt.
4. The census advance requires an appropriation. Other administration has real costs, addressed through lawful appropriations and cost-based fees. Repayment and a return are objectives, not guaranteed revenue. Tax treatment can also affect public receipts.
5. Cannabis receives an express, bounded new federal pathway. The bill preserves federal food-and-drug requirements and does not itself approve cannabis products for medical use.
6. Psilocybin and ibogaine compacts remain conditional on a lawful federal pathway. The draft does not override FDA approval, investigational requirements, or controlled-substance law for those therapies. That is a deliberate limit: it does not immediately open a general commercial therapy market. [19]
7. Neither the book nor an individual author becomes the official historical authority. Their research can supply independently assessed evidence. The book’s continuing factual work is not represented as complete by the bill.
8. Participation does not automatically release unrelated restitution, property, or civil-rights claims. “Full and final restitution” in policy rhetoric has not been converted into an involuntary legal release.

## Numerical drafting defaults

These are proposed implementation choices for a complete first draft, not figures previously supplied or approved by the policy author. They can be changed without abandoning the governmental model.

| Provision | Version 1.0 default |
|---|---|
| Initial historical roster | Within 18 months after funds become available |
| Roster comment period | At least 180 days |
| Founding enrollment | 24 months, and at least 6 months after revised roster publication |
| Certification of a complete application or compact | 180 days |
| Public comment on a proposed exclusive market | At least 90 days |
| Maximum initial exclusive term | 25 years; renewal requires reassessment and agreement |
| Founding Fund recovery target | Audited net disbursements plus a one-time 5 percent return |
| Repayment assessment | 1 percent of the nation's economic share of positive qualifying net income until the aggregate target is reached |
| New exclusive-compact authority | 15 years, with one additional year for timely pending applications |
| Institutional and performance review | Every 5 years |

No dollar appropriation has been invented. Section 1101 requires an estimate and an actual appropriation before spending. Congressional scoring is needed for the advance, administrative expenses, proposed tax treatment, contingent recoveries, and other fiscal effects.
